# Togal.AI — competitor verdict (research current to 26 September 2026)

**Verdict: Togal.AI is a credible, premium-priced AI takeoff competitor, not a complete estimating system.** Its strongest proof is a focused workflow: cloud plan management, AI-assisted detection/measurement/counting, revision comparison, collaboration, search and export. Its public Growth price is **US$299 per user per month, billed yearly**, with unlimited automated takeoffs and chat prompts; teams of four or more are pushed to a custom Business plan [1]. That price and the absence of a public self-serve free tier support KindAI’s decision to remove a cheap A$9 trial. However, Togal’s own demo explicitly says it does not replace the estimator and leaves valuation, bid amounts and proposals to the user [2]. KindAI should therefore compete against Togal on the **takeoff-to-priced-estimate workflow and Australian trade context**, not on generic “AI takeoff” claims.

## What Togal sells and how it positions itself

Togal describes itself as “an AI-powered takeoff tool built by estimators” that detects, measures and compares drawings, claiming “up to 98% accuracy” and takeoffs “5x faster” [3]. Its feature page presents a three-step workflow: upload and auto-name plans; execute manual or AI takeoff; collaborate and share in real time [4]. The product includes Togal Button automation, image/text/pattern search, auto-naming, Togal.CHAT for questions and scope/RFI drafting, manual editing, revision comparison, cloud permissions and export to Excel or marked-up PDF [4].

The trade positioning is broad rather than Australia-specific. Togal has dedicated pages for drywall, electrical, FF&E, general contracting, landscaping, mechanical, painting, plumbing and glass/glazing/windows [5]. The message is operational: “faster takeoffs,” “cloud-based collaboration,” and AI that reduces repetitive clicking and counting. Togal’s homepage names many professional-builder customers, including DPR, Consigli, Clark Construction and Christman, but these are vendor-published logos and should be treated as claimed customer evidence, not independently verified adoption [3].

Togal’s conversion path is sales-assisted. The demo page asks prospects to book a live takeoff using their own plans: “No generic walkthroughs. No canned examples. Just your plans, quantified in minutes” [6]. Pricing says “Try Now” but routes to the demo, and the FAQ explains signup as booking a demo rather than creating an unrestricted account [1]. GetApp Australia reports **no free trial available**, **zero user reviews**, and lists support through email/help desk, knowledge base and chat [7]. The practical implication is a high-intent, proof-before-purchase funnel rather than a low-friction freemium funnel.

## Pricing, packaging and product economics

The visible entry price is **US$299/user/month on annual billing**. Growth includes unlimited automated takeoffs, unlimited chat prompts, unlimited image/symbol searches and internal and external collaboration [1]. Business is custom for teams with 4+ users and adds onboarding/training, dedicated support, classification-library templates, quantity discounts, security compliance and SSO [1]. Togal also says it offers multi-license discounts and enterprise solutions through sales [1].

This packaging is materially different from KindAI’s configured usage tiers. Togal sells essentially unlimited usage at a high per-seat price; KindAI’s current structure sells bounded AI capacity (15, 50 or 150 takeoffs) with low seat counts. Togal demonstrates that buyers may accept premium pricing when the product is framed as a production estimating tool, but it does **not** prove that unlimited AI is required. A premium KindAI offer can retain minimal free AI usage and charge for reliable monthly capacity, provided the product makes the output useful beyond raw quantities.

Togal’s product scope is narrower than the word “estimating” may imply. In the February 2026 live demo, the presenter said: **“We are not replacing an estimator... Togal.AI will not do your entire takeoff for you, it is a dance between man and machine.”** The analysis also stated that Togal’s “bread and butter is getting these quantities” and that valuations for bid amounts remain with the user [2]. The demo showed an export-to-Excel flow, not a native priced estimate, bid or proposal workflow [2]. An independent 2026 comparison similarly characterises Togal as fast AI-assisted takeoff and says buyers wanting priced estimates, bid preparation, procurement or supplier-quote comparison may need another system [8]. This is the most actionable competitive gap: quantity extraction is valuable, but it is not the whole commercial estimating job.

## Demonstrated workflow and integration evidence

The official February 20, 2026 video was a 38-minute full-length live demo with 11,982 views at capture [9]. The demonstrated flow included cloud workspaces with Editor/Viewer permissions, multiple users editing one takeoff, Togal Chat reading manuals/specifications, automated room polygons, wall centre-lines and count pins, auto-classification, custom material libraries, 3D checking, text/image/pattern search, drawing-revision overlays and export to Excel/marked-up PDF [2] [9]. In one displayed plan, the tool generated 50 target areas, 124 target lines and 329 target counts, including 17,797 square feet of net area, 91 door centre-lines, 290 linear feet, 89 single-swing doors, 2 double doors, 37 toilets and 62 sinks [2]. These are demo outputs, not an accuracy benchmark.

The same evidence shows the human-review burden. AI-generated objects still require review and mapping to the user’s classification/material library; the presenter did not claim full material-type inference. The product can refine false positives manually, and final commercial use still depends on the estimator’s judgement [2]. This matches independent estimator discussion. One Reddit user wrote that after a demo and test, Togal “does simplify the takeoff process for some scopes ... but everything needs to be double checked,” adding that it was more comfortable to do the work themselves [10]. Another user reported that it was useful for area takeoffs and web-based collaboration but said, “I would not use it at this point for a hard bid” [10].

There is meaningful integration evidence, but it is selective. Togal’s Stevens Construction case study says its native Ediphi integration maps quantities into Ediphi Unit Price Catalog items, removing export/re-entry and manual reconciliation. Stevens reports a four-person preconstruction team matching the estimate output of much larger GC departments; the case study is vendor-published and the performance claim is not independently audited [11]. eTakeoff separately describes a partnership in which Togal is an add-on for architectural floor plans, while eTakeoff’s own SnapAI covers vector-PDF measurement and can complement drawings Togal cannot accommodate [12]. GetApp Australia lists OnScreen Guidance and eTakeoff as integrations and otherwise shows no integrations found [7]. This suggests an integration-led enterprise route, but not a broad Australian accounting, CRM or pricing-data ecosystem.

## Buyer voice: useful signal, not broad market proof

The strongest recurring buyer concern is not whether AI can accelerate the first pass; it is whether the remaining review work destroys the saving. In a Reddit thread, an estimator described Togal as getting “80% of the way done,” then said the last 20% required disproportionate manual cleanup and verification. The same commenter concluded that delegating the mental mapping to AI produced “a very small reduction in my actual estimating time” on their work [13]. Another commenter said sales videos showed “super clean architectural plans” and asked whether the system could handle lighting fixtures and control devices on electrical plans [13].

A separate 2024 Reddit discussion provides a balanced positive signal. A user said the web-based framework and multi-user takeoff were among the best parts, while another customer said the tool was useful for walls, areas and counts but not for a hard bid; they also praised document storage and collaboration [10]. These are real practitioner observations, but the threads are North American and self-selected. They are evidence of objections and use cases, not evidence of Australian product-market fit.

> “It does simplify the takeoff process for some scopes ... but everything needs to be double checked.” [10]

> “I would not use it at this point for a hard bid.” [10]

> “Their demos are tailored to clean and structured drawings.” [13]

These complaints point to a specific KindAI requirement: show messy, real trade plans and quantify the review path. Do not promise full automation. A buyer needs to see what is detected, what is uncertain, how corrections are made, and how the corrected quantities become priced line items and a defensible quote.

## Australian market demand and route-to-market relevance

Official Australian data establishes a large construction base but does **not** verify demand for Togal-like estimating software. Master Builders Australia reports **478,651 construction businesses**, **1.38 million people employed**, **$351.3 billion of building and construction work delivered in 2025–26**, and **98.6% of construction businesses employing fewer than 20 people** [14]. ABS’s 2024–25 Australian Industry release reports construction employment of **1.313 million**, industry value added of **A$191.751 billion**, and construction operating profit before tax of **A$63.410 billion** [15]. These are total-industry indicators, not the serviceable market for AI estimating software. They should not be converted into a software TAM without segmentation, willingness-to-pay data and verified buyer research.

Search-demand evidence is similarly limited. Google Trends is a valid tool for location/time comparisons, but the accessible evidence in this research did not produce a stable, publishable Australia series for “Togal AI.” Togal-branded searches should therefore be treated as likely low-volume and unsuitable as a standalone acquisition thesis. Generic terms such as “construction takeoff software,” “AI takeoff,” and “estimating software Australia” are more commercially meaningful, but no keyword-volume number is asserted here without a verifiable current export. The honest conclusion is that current evidence supports a category hypothesis, not quantified Australian search demand.

The clearest verified channel route is Master Builders Australia. Its official partnership page says it represents builders, specialist contractors and supporting businesses, and offers event sponsorships, corporate subscriptions, targeted media/advertising, magazine placements, data access and events [16]. It reports **32,000 members represented through state and territory associations** and **125,000+ engaged audience across national digital channels**; these are the association’s own access metrics, not a guaranteed KindAI reach [16]. Access difficulty is **medium-to-high**: the route is real and official, but it is a paid partnership/sponsorship conversation rather than an open marketplace listing. Togal’s eTakeoff partnership demonstrates another route: embed or bundle AI into an incumbent takeoff workflow [12]. For KindAI, that proves partnership is a credible distribution mechanism, not that any specific Australian partner will accept the product.

## Weaknesses, risks and implications for KindAI

**Accuracy and review risk.** Togal markets up to 98% floor-plan accuracy, but the public evidence does not define the test set, scope types, denominator or independent validation [3] [6]. Independent users repeatedly stress double-checking and weak performance on messy or non-standard plans [10] [13]. KindAI should avoid unsupported accuracy or time-saving claims and instead publish scope-level evaluation with review time and error categories.

**Takeoff-to-estimate gap.** Togal’s own demo frames the product as quantities plus human judgement, with valuation and bid amounts left to the user [2]. This leaves a defensible differentiation space for a product that connects quantities to Australian rates, assemblies, exclusions, markups and quote-ready outputs. The evidence only supports making that a product direction; it does not support claiming KindAI already does it.

**Premium pricing and annual commitment.** US$299 per user/month billed yearly is a significant commitment for small Australian firms, especially when 98.6% of construction businesses have fewer than 20 employees [1] [14]. Yet Togal’s price also validates premium positioning for production users. KindAI should not respond with A$9 trial pricing; a controlled, paid, founder-assisted evaluation with one or a few real plans is more consistent with the category’s trust problem.

**Demo-selection risk.** Reddit users specifically distrust clean-plan demos [13]. Togal’s own demo uses a structured walkthrough and visibly retains manual review [2]. KindAI’s competitive proof should include difficult Australian plan sets, revision changes, trade-specific symbols and an explicit audit trail. This recommendation is limited to what Togal evidence proves: demo credibility and review transparency matter.

**Geographic and standards risk.** Togal’s published customer and case-study evidence is overwhelmingly US/international, and its eTakeoff page references AIA measurement standards [12]. Nothing reviewed proves Australian NCC, state tender, GST, metric-rate, subcontractor or local workflow coverage. KindAI should treat Australian localisation as an evidence-building opportunity, not assume Togal is weak in every local workflow.

## Specific go-to-market implications from Togal only

1. **Keep the premium posture.** Togal publicly anchors AI takeoff at US$299 per user/month annual billing and uses demos rather than an unrestricted free product [1] [6]. This supports removing a cheap A$9 trial and using a paid or tightly capped evaluation.
2. **Sell a measurable business job, not “AI.”** Togal wins attention with takeoff speed, but its own demo concedes the estimator remains responsible for the final work [2]. Position the offer around a verified quote workflow, reviewability and Australian context; do not claim automation that has not been tested.
3. **Use a real-plan evaluation.** Mirror Togal’s “your plans” demo approach [6], but make the acceptance test explicit: upload one representative plan set, document detected quantities, corrections, review time and final export. Keep AI usage minimal and purposeful.
4. **Target small teams and trade specialists first.** Australian construction is dominated by sub-20-person firms [14]. Togal’s team/enterprise packaging and broad trade pages show that collaboration and vertical workflows matter [1] [5]. A narrow trade proof is more credible than a generic builder message.
5. **Pursue association and incumbent partnerships carefully.** Master Builders offers official event, media and partnership pathways [16], while eTakeoff shows an AI add-on partnership model [12]. Treat both as sales conversations with real access costs, not guaranteed distribution.
