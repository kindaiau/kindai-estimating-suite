# Kreo — competitor verdict (research date: 26 September 2026)

**Verdict: Kreo is a credible premium benchmark for AI-assisted takeoff, but not proof that an Australian tradie-focused estimating product can win on “AI accuracy” alone.** Its current offer spans a low-cost, non-AI entry tier through a high-priced AI Pro tier and custom enterprise work. The strongest evidence is its broad workflow: cloud drawing management, manual and AI measurement, assemblies/rates, spreadsheet-like reports, Excel/PDF export, collaboration, API and enterprise automation. The strongest counter-evidence is that Kreo’s own product demo still requires scale setup, precise element selection, confidence/similarity tuning and manual correction; the demo explicitly warns that Auto Measure is intended for architectural floor plans and can be “messy and incorrect” on structural plans.[1][2] For KindAI’s premium direction, Kreo supports charging for a controlled estimating workflow and human-reviewed outputs, not offering unlimited cheap AI usage.

## What Kreo sells now

Kreo positions itself as browser-based AI takeoff and estimating for quantity surveyors, estimators and contractors. Its current site says the workflow is: upload PDF, CAD or image files; let AI detect, measure and count elements or work manually; then export reports, bills of quantities or cost estimates.[1] The AI surface includes Auto Measure, One-click Area, Auto Count, AI scale detection, automatic classification, cross-sheet search and Caddie, described as an agent that reads drawings and generates measurements. Kreo also offers 2D and 3D views, drawing comparison, contextual comments, role permissions, live-linked grid/spreadsheet reports and an items/assemblies database that can convert measurements into materials, labour and equipment costs.[1][3]

Its commercial positioning is notably broader than a simple takeoff utility. Kreo claims “over 1,000 clients” on its AI takeoff solution page and displays a customer list including Cast Consultancy, John Lewis, Acuity Brands, Artelia, CEF, Willmott Dixon, IMEG, PC Construction and Viva Energy.[3] Those are vendor-reported logos/listings, not independently verified customer counts or Australian adoption. The homepage also displays ratings of 5/5 from 24 Google reviews, 4.5/5 from 32 G2 reviews and 4.4/5 from 25 Capterra reviews.[1] These ratings are useful positioning evidence but should not be treated as a statistically representative market sample.

### Pricing, trial and packaging

The public pricing page is currency-localised. The US-dollar display lists Lite at **US$35/user/month billed annually**, Plus at **US$70/user/month billed annually**, Pro at **US$175/user/month billed annually**, and Enterprise as custom.[1] The GBP display lists £25, £50 and £125 per user/month respectively. The page says Plus and Pro have a **7-day trial**, a 7-day money-back/refund window, and Enterprise can receive a **3-month trial** by arrangement. It also says Pro is billed annually per user at £125/month and offers USD, GBP and EUR selection. No Australian-dollar price was published in the captured page, so an AUD comparison should not be fabricated.

The packaging creates a clear premium anchor. Lite is for collaboration without AI-powered takeoff tools; Plus adds project templates, assemblies, reports and Excel import/export; Pro is labelled “Most popular” and adds Auto Measure, One-click Area, Auto Count, Caddie, custom report templates, API (sales-assisted), wand/dropper tools and premium support. Enterprise adds database migration, advanced permissions, full API/integration, AI workflow automation, multi-page launch and a dedicated success manager.[1]

This is relevant to KindAI’s configured tiers. Kreo prices by seat and makes the AI capability a reason to move up to Pro, rather than publishing an inexpensive unlimited AI plan. KindAI’s A$149 Sole Tradie, A$450 Pro, A$1,499 Mid-Tier Builder and custom Enterprise tiers can therefore be positioned around workflow scope, seats, governance and included AI capacity. Kreo does not prove those exact prices or takeoff quotas; it proves that a visible premium ladder with a short trial and sales-assisted enterprise motion is a live category pattern.

## Capability evidence and product friction

An official Kreo onboarding video (published 11 September 2024) demonstrates a complete workflow: project and folder setup, user/guest permissions, assemblies database, drawing upload, scale setting, manual polygons/rectangles/polylines, One-click Area, Auto Count, Auto Measure, reports, Excel export and PDF export.[2] The video is first-hand product evidence rather than a review, but its limitations matter:

- One-click Area may require extra points or an exclusion point when the first click is wrong (around 09:03).[2]
- Auto Count depends on selecting the target element precisely and adjusting a similarity-percentage slider to reduce interference from surrounding geometry (around 09:33–09:44).[2]
- The presenter says Auto Measure is compatible only with architectural floor plans, especially residential plans, and “should not be used with structural plans as the results may be messy and incorrect” (around 10:25–10:48).[2]
- Auto Measure 2.0 may require manual AI-confidence adjustment or “Fix with AI” when the prompt result is unsatisfactory (around 11:12).[2]
- The workflow still starts with setting the scale, either manually or with AI assistance (around 06:48).[2]

Kreo’s 28 August 2026 Auto Measure 3.0 announcement claims a second review pass and reports vendor-tested improvements against labelled ground truth: outline accuracy from 72% to 94%, area captured from 92.6% to 99.3%, results-kept from 81% to 90%, coverage from 81% to 87%, and narrow-span score from 0.42 to 0.67.[4] These are useful current product claims, but they are not an independent benchmark, and access is “by request.” The page says the test set used complex, noisy plans and that results were compared with labelled ground truth, but it does not publish the dataset, class mix, Australian plan mix or an external audit.[4] KindAI should therefore avoid claiming parity or superiority from Kreo’s numbers.

The main implication is that buyers are paying for a reviewable production system, not magic automation. Kreo’s own evidence supports messaging around faster first-pass measurement plus controlled checking, rather than promising an estimate that needs no estimator oversight.

## Customers, integrations and routes to market

Kreo’s customer proof is strongest in professional and mid-market/enterprise environments. Its site names consultancy, contractor, engineering, manufacturer and builder organisations, and its case-study page frames the outcome as shifting teams from manual measurement to higher-value analysis, client work and site management.[1][5] The case-study page does not provide a sufficiently detailed, independently audited Australian ROI dataset; it should be read as vendor positioning.

Kreo has a formal partner route with three categories: **technology partners**, **academic partners** and **reseller partners**. The page names ConWize, Contractor Foreman and Arizona State University, and asks prospective partners to submit name, email and a description.[6] This verifies a route but not partner reach, revenue or Australian distribution. Access difficulty is therefore **moderate**: the route is public and application-led, but no acceptance criteria, commission schedule, member count or Australian partner list is published. A KindAI analogue would need a concrete local integration or reseller proposition, not an assumption that a partner page creates distribution.

Kreo also has an Autodesk Marketplace publisher profile. Autodesk describes Kreo as a cloud AEC/BIM platform covering takeoff, cost estimating, scheduling and bidding; it lists Kreo Plan for 5D cost estimates/4D schedules and Kreo Design for BIM/CDE workflows.[7] Marketplace presence is a credible ecosystem signal and a route to discovery among Autodesk users, but the profile shows no review volume or Australian reach. Kreo’s 8 December 2022 Contractor Foreman announcement says the integration supports exporting Kreo takeoff data into estimates, invoices, bills and orders, with aligned cost items, rates, rules, formulas and cost codes; it explicitly says the solution suits small and medium contractors in the US and Canada.[8] That last point is important: the verified integration route is not evidence of Australian localisation.

## Australian market context and buyer voice

The Australian Bureau of Statistics reported on 24 June 2025 that construction represented **7.0% of GDP** in 2023–24 and employed around **1.3 million people**. Total construction income was **A$633.6 billion**, comprising A$235.2b building construction, A$122.4b heavy/civil engineering and A$276.1b construction services.[9] Construction services—where many tradies and subcontractors sit—generated A$276.1b, with A$116.6b of income from subcontracting arrangements. These are total construction-industry figures, not the addressable market or verified demand for estimating software. No source reviewed here supports converting them into a software TAM or claiming that a given percentage is actively shopping for AI takeoff.

Australian-relevant independent review evidence is available through Capterra Australia. Its Kreo profile reports 4.4/5 from 25 reviews, 96% positive sentiment, a starting price of GBP95 and availability in Australia, Canada, the UK and US.[10] Capterra identifies itself as using verified reviews and independent research, while also disclosing that vendors may pay for sponsored profiles or referral traffic.[10] The review sample includes an Australian filter but the accessible page does not establish how many of the 25 reviews are Australian; do not represent the full sample as Australian buyer proof.

The review quotes show both value and objections:

> “The best thing about Kreo is the AI assisted take off. I find auto measure can be a little messy and i spend as much time organizing the data as i would have doing a normal takeoff.” — verified UK construction business-development reviewer, 21 November 2023.[10]

> “Kreo works best with vector drawings and modern building floorplans… it’s difficult to get the most out of the ‘automagical’ features” on scanned condominium plans. — Canadian consultant/appraiser, 22 May 2024.[10]

> “Multiple line items (conditions) for the same take off… each time you start and stop, a new condition is created.” — US estimator, 30 May 2024.[10]

> “The fact Kreo is cloud based makes it difficult during days in which the wifi may be down.” — UK estimator, 14 September 2023.[10]

A negative review is also present: a UK quantity surveyor wrote that it was “very bad” and “not user-friendly” after spending a day without producing a day’s worth of measurements.[10] These are individual experiences, not broad market proof, but they expose exactly the objections a premium Australian product must handle: source-plan quality, false or messy detections, repetitive line-item cleanup, connectivity and onboarding.

Reddit buyer voice is thinner and less Australia-specific. In r/estimators, a user with three years’ experience using PlanSwift and Excel asked: “I’ve been looking at KREO. Is it worth it?” while seeking starter-software recommendations.[11] Another estimator-market-research post asks for tools to measure perimeters, walls, partitions, doors, windows and floors so the team can focus on costs “instead of clicking,” and says precision need not be perfect; the post sought a tool available in Europe, not Australia.[12] These posts prove active evaluation language and the job-to-be-done, but they do **not** prove Australian willingness to pay, conversion intent or category-wide demand.

## Search demand: what can and cannot be claimed

Google’s official Trends documentation says Trends is a sampled, anonymised and aggregated view of Google searches; values are normalised to a 0–100 relative scale, and very-low-volume terms can appear as “0.” Google also warns that Trends is not a poll and should be one data point among others.[13] The current public search evidence reviewed here does not provide a defensible numeric Australian trend series for “Kreo” or “Kreo estimating software.” Accordingly, Kreo-branded search demand should be treated as **low/uncertain**, not as a validated acquisition channel. Broader terms such as construction estimating software and takeoff software require a separate Keyword Planner/Trends extraction before making volume or CAC decisions. Do not infer demand from search snippets or from Kreo’s social follower counts.

## Go-to-market implications specific to Kreo

1. **Premium positioning is supported, but sell the controlled workflow.** Kreo’s Pro tier, annual per-user pricing, API upsell and enterprise customisation show that AI can be packaged as a professional workflow rather than a cheap trial commodity.[1] KindAI can retain minimal free AI usage and make paid value the ability to complete repeatable estimates with review, reports, collaboration and support.
2. **Make source-plan compatibility a front-door qualification step.** Kreo’s own demo warning on structural plans and independent complaints about scans make file quality and plan type a real conversion risk.[2][10] A KindAI offer should specify supported Australian plan formats and show what happens when AI confidence is low, rather than imply universal accuracy.
3. **Use a bounded proof offer, not unlimited free AI.** Kreo uses a 7-day trial for Plus/Pro and a sales-assisted/3-month enterprise trial.[1] A relevant KindAI entry offer is a short, tightly capped proof using one real plan and a human-review checklist, with a clear conversion to the A$149 Sole Tradie or A$450 Pro tier. This is a recommendation based on Kreo’s trial structure and observed AI correction burden, not evidence that a particular KindAI trial will convert.
4. **Differentiate on Australian estimating context, not generic AI claims.** Kreo’s verified Contractor Foreman integration is described for US and Canadian contractors, and its Autodesk profile is global.[7][8] This leaves a credible wedge for Australian workflow details, local trade conventions, export formats and support, but no source here proves a specific missing Australian integration. Validate each claimed local advantage with customer interviews before publishing it.
5. **Treat partnerships as an access experiment.** Kreo’s public technology, academic and reseller programmes show the route exists, while its public page provides no reach or acceptance data.[6] The specific lesson is to pursue one measurable Australian channel—such as a construction education partner, estimating consultant or software integration—and track qualified demos and paid conversions. Do not forecast distribution from membership or partner counts that have not been published.

## Key risks and caveats

- Kreo’s pricing is displayed in USD/GBP/EUR, not AUD, and currency conversion would be a point-in-time estimate rather than a published Australian price.[1]
- AI accuracy figures are self-reported product-update claims; they are current and numerically specific but not independently audited.[4]
- Vendor-reported “1,000 clients,” logos, ratings and case-study outcomes are not proof of Australian customers or retention.[1][3][5]
- Independent review evidence is small, mixed-country and subject to the review platform’s disclosed commercial model.[10]
- Buyer complaints are individual reports, not prevalence estimates. They should guide product qualification and demos, not be generalised into universal Kreo failure.[10]
- ABS construction income and employment describe the total industry, not verified demand for estimating software.[9]
- No defensible current Australian search-volume number for Kreo was established. Google Trends low-volume zeros and relative indices must not be presented as market size.[13]
