Scanning Best Practices
-----------------------

The quality of your scan directly affects the accuracy of your takeoff. Here's how to get the best results from each method.

### Office Scanner (Recommended)

Best Quality

1. 1Place plans face-down on the scanner glass
2. 2Set resolution to 200–300 DPI
3. 3Select PDF output (not JPG)
4. 4Save to email or shared drive
5. 5Upload directly to Kindai

Tip: Most office MFPs (Ricoh, Konica, Canon, HP) produce excellent results at 200 DPI. No need to go higher — it just creates larger files.

### Phone Camera

Quick & Easy

1. 1Lay the plan flat on a clean, well-lit surface
2. 2Hold phone directly above the plan (not at an angle)
3. 3Avoid shadows — use natural light or overhead lighting
4. 4Use the highest camera resolution setting
5. 5For A0/A1 plans, photograph in sections

Tip: iPhone and modern Android cameras produce excellent results. The key is avoiding shadows and keeping the phone parallel to the plan surface.

### CAD / Digital Export

Highest Accuracy

1. 1In AutoCAD, Revit, or ArchiCAD: File → Export → PDF
2. 2Select 'High Quality Print' or equivalent
3. 3Include all relevant drawing sheets
4. 4Upload the exported PDF directly

Tip: PDF exports from CAD software are the highest quality input. Kindai can read every line, symbol, and annotation with maximum accuracy.

### Large-Format Scanner

Enterprise

1. 1Use HP DesignJet, Contex, or similar large-format scanner
2. 2Set to 200 DPI minimum (300 DPI for complex drawings)
3. 3Export as PDF — not TIFF
4. 4Split very large files into logical sections if over 32MB

Tip: Large-format scanners produce the best results for A0/A1 engineering drawings. If your scan is over 32MB, reduce DPI to 200 — Kindai reads it just as accurately.

File Format Guide
-----------------

| Format | Best For | Max Size | Quality |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PDFRecommended | Office scans, CAD exports, multi-page sets | 32MB | Excellent |
| JPG / JPEG | Phone photos, scanned images | 32MB | Very Good |
| PNG | Screenshots, digital drawings | 32MB | Excellent |
| WebP | Web-optimised images | 32MB | Very Good |

Note: HEIC files (iPhone default format) are not directly supported. iPhones automatically convert to JPG when you share or email a photo — just share the photo to yourself first, then upload the JPG.

Enterprise Workflow
-------------------

For companies with full drawing sets (20–50 pages), here's the recommended end-to-end workflow.

01

### Gather your drawing set

Collect all relevant plan sheets: architectural, structural, electrical, hydraulic, mechanical, hardware schedules. No need to sort them — Kindai handles mixed drawing types.

02

### Scan or export to PDF

Use your office scanner or CAD export. Aim for 200–300 DPI. Save as PDF. One file per drawing sheet is fine — Kindai accepts up to 50 pages per job.

03

### Upload all pages at once

Drag and drop all your PDF/image files into the upload zone. Kindai uploads them in parallel and shows you a page list with upload status for each file.

04

### Select your trade and set pricing

Choose the relevant trade from the dropdown. Set your markup percentage and labour rate. Kindai uses your saved rates automatically if you have a trade profile set up.

05

### Generate the takeoff

Hit Generate. Kindai processes your pages in batches of 5, analyses each batch with GPT-4 Vision, then merges all results into a single combined takeoff. For 50 pages, expect 2–4 minutes.

06

### Review and export

Review the materials list, check the confidence score, and adjust any items as needed. Export to PDF or send directly to your quoting software via the export options.

Tips for Maximum Accuracy
-------------------------

### Do this

* Use PDF format wherever possible
* Scan at 200–300 DPI minimum
* Upload all pages for a job together
* Add a job description for extra context
* Select the correct trade before generating
* Review the confidence score on results

### Avoid this

* Blurry or out-of-focus photos
* Plans photographed at an angle
* Very dark or shadowed images
* Files over 32MB (reduce DPI instead)
* Uploading unrelated drawings in the same job
* Ignoring a low confidence score without reviewing
